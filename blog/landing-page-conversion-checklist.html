The Landing Page Checklist That Lifts Conversions

Digital Picto October 29, 2026 9 min read

A landing page should answer the exact question the ad asked, above the fold, in the same words. Page quality also affects how often your ads serve at all, which means a weak page costs you twice: fewer conversions and fewer impressions. We build the page before the campaign for this reason.

Does the page continue the ad's promise?

The headline should repeat the offer the ad made, in the same language the searcher used.

A click is a question. If the visitor has to re-orient to work out whether they are in the right place, most will not bother.

This is the most common cause of clicks without conversions, and it costs nothing to fix.

The headline has one job: confirm the visitor is in the right place. A visitor who clicked an ad about a specific problem should see that problem named in the first line, in the words they used, not a brand statement.

Everything else above the fold supports that confirmation. One proof element, one clear action, and nothing that requires scrolling to understand what the page is for.

Is there one action, clearly?

One page, one job. Competing calls to action split attention and reduce both.

Navigation menus, secondary offers and 'learn more' links all give a visitor a way to leave without converting.

If the page needs to serve two intents, it needs to be two pages.

Form length is a negotiation, not a best practice. Each field costs completions and buys qualification, so the right number depends on whether your constraint is lead volume or lead quality.

Ask only what you will use before the first conversation. Fields collected because a CRM has a column for them are pure cost, and the information was available by asking on the call.

Does page quality affect ad delivery?

Yes, on both Google and Meta. A better page serves more often at a lower cost.

In a US health insurance campaign we built the landing page and site first, before keyword work, specifically to raise page quality score and win more impressions.

That campaign reached a 9.90% conversion rate and 8.72% click-through rate in one of the most competitive categories on the platform.

Proof has to be specific to be worth the space. A named client, a real figure or an identifiable photograph of work does something; a row of generic logos and a five-star graphic does not.

Place it next to the decision. Proof at the bottom of the page is read by people who already decided; proof beside the form is read by the ones still deciding.

Is the form asking for too much?

Ask for what you need to qualify and nothing else. Every additional field costs completions.

Name, contact and one qualifying question is usually enough to route an enquiry properly. Budget and timeline can be asked on the call.

Where qualification genuinely matters, ask the qualifying question first so the visitor invests before reaching the contact fields.

The page should load before the visitor decides whether to wait, which on mobile data means the headline and form rendering almost immediately. This is why we treat load behaviour as part of the checklist rather than a technical afterthought.

It also feeds the landing page experience component of Quality Score, which means the same work lowers the cost of the traffic arriving at the page.

What should you remove?

Navigation, competing offers, and anything that invites exploration.

A landing page is not a website. Site navigation gives a visitor who was about to convert four other places to go, and the usual destination is the homepage, from which almost nobody returns.

The same applies to secondary calls to action. Two offers on one page do not double conversion; they split attention and reduce it.

How do you know which change worked?

Change one element at a time and give it enough traffic to read.

The temptation after a rebuild is to change headline, form, proof and layout together, which produces a result nobody can attribute or repeat.

Start with the headline, because it is the cheapest to change and usually the largest effect. Then the form, then the proof. Layout last, because it is the most work for the least reliable gain.
