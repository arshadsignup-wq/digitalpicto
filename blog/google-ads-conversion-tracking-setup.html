Google Ads Conversion Tracking: GTM and GA4 Setup

Digital Picto October 18, 2026 10 min read

Conversion tracking is the first thing to fix in any Google Ads account, because every optimisation decision after it depends on the signal being accurate. In two accounts we rebuilt, tracking was repaired before anything was judged. One moved ROI up 87%, the other recovered from spending nothing at all. Neither result came from clever bidding; both came from being able to see what was actually happening.

Why does conversion tracking come before optimisation?

Because an account measuring the wrong thing will optimise confidently in the wrong direction.

Google's automated bidding strategies take your conversion signal as the target. If that signal is firing on the wrong page, double-counting, or missing entirely, the algorithm will spend your budget pursuing it anyway.

When we rebuilt a sauna retailer's account, the symptom was high cost per click and no conversions. The cause was that nothing was being measured reliably, so every previous optimisation had been made on noise. With clean tracking in place, the restructure produced 2,416 conversions and $214,630 in tracked conversion value.

This is the least glamorous hour in paid search and consistently the highest-return one.

Verify with a real submission, not with the tag debugger alone. Submit the form as a visitor would, then confirm the conversion appears in the interface and the enquiry appears wherever enquiries actually land.

Those two checks catch different faults. The debugger confirms the tag fires; the inbox confirms the thing the tag claims happened actually happened.

What should you track as a conversion?

Track the action that indicates genuine buying intent, not every interaction a visitor can have.

A form submission, a booked appointment, a phone call over a sensible duration, or a completed purchase are all defensible conversions. A page view, a scroll, or a click on the contact link usually are not.

Counting weak actions as conversions inflates your numbers and teaches the bidding algorithm to chase people who browse. If you must track soft signals, keep them as secondary conversions so they inform reporting without steering bidding.

Double counting is the most common corruption and the hardest to notice, because the account simply looks successful. A tag on both the form and the thank-you page, or a page that can be refreshed, will inflate every number downstream.

Set the counting method deliberately. One conversion per click for lead generation, every conversion for ecommerce, and the default is not always the one you want.

How do Google Tag Manager and GA4 fit together?

Tag Manager deploys the tags; GA4 collects and models the behaviour; Google Ads imports the conversions it should bid toward.

Tag Manager gives you one container to manage every tag, so you are not editing site code each time a conversion changes. GA4 then becomes the single definition of an event, which both Ads and your reporting can reference.

The setup we use on managed accounts installs Tag Manager, defines the events in GA4, and imports the specific events that represent real buying intent into Google Ads as conversion actions. Our paid advertising service treats this as part of onboarding rather than an extra.

Mark exactly one conversion action as primary, and make it the one with money behind it. Secondary actions are useful for reporting and will quietly distort bidding if promoted.

Review that list quarterly. Accounts accumulate conversion actions from old tests, and a stale primary action is enough to misdirect a smart bidding campaign for months.

How do you know tracking is actually working?

Compare what the platform reports against what your CRM or inbox actually received. If they disagree, the platform is wrong.

Use Tag Assistant and GA4 DebugView to confirm events fire on the right action and only once. Then reconcile a week of reported conversions against real enquiries.

A discrepancy is not a rounding issue; it is the problem to solve before you judge any campaign. Accounts that look like they have a bidding problem very often have a counting problem.

Reconcile against the system of record weekly for the first month. If the platform reports more conversions than your CRM received, the gap is a tracking fault, not a reporting delay, and every optimisation decision until it is fixed will be wrong.

This reconciliation is the first thing we do on an inherited account, before touching a single bid or keyword.

What breaks conversion tracking most often?

Duplicated tags, conversions firing on page load rather than on the thank-you action, and tracking that was never updated after a site change.

A site redesign that changes the confirmation URL will silently stop conversions being recorded, and the account will look like it collapsed overnight.

Build a habit of re-verifying tracking after any site change, and before drawing conclusions about a sudden drop in performance.

What about phone calls?

Track them, and track them separately from forms.

For most service businesses calls are the majority of conversions and the least measured. Call conversions with a minimum duration filter out the misdials and the wrong numbers.

Keep calls as their own conversion action so you can see which campaigns produce which behaviour. A campaign that generates calls and no forms looks like a failure under a form-only setup.

Does enhanced conversions matter?

It improves match rates, and it needs a deliberate decision about data.

Sending hashed first-party data recovers attribution the browser loses, which shows up as more conversions credited to campaigns that earned them.

Treat it as a data decision, not a toggle. Send what you have a basis to send, document it, and make sure your privacy policy reflects what the site actually does.

Frequently asked questions

They serve different jobs. GA4 measures behaviour across the site; Google Ads needs conversion actions to bid toward. Most accounts import GA4 events into Ads so there is one definition of a conversion.

They use different attribution models and lookback windows. Ads credits the ad interaction; GA4 may credit a different channel. Small differences are normal; large ones usually indicate a tagging fault.

You can spend money, but you cannot optimise. Automated bidding strategies require a conversion signal, and without one you are buying clicks with no way to tell which were worth buying.

A straightforward setup through Tag Manager and GA4 is typically a few hours of work plus a verification period of several days to confirm events fire correctly in live traffic.

If your account is spending without a clear conversion picture, start there rather than with bids. It is usually the cheapest fix available.
