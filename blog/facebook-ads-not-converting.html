Why Your Facebook Ads Get Clicks But No Leads

By Digital Picto October 16, 2026 8 min read

If your Facebook Ads get clicks but no leads, the fault is rarely the ads themselves. In the accounts we have rebuilt, the cause is almost always one of four things: conversion tracking that is not recording properly, a landing page that does not continue the promise the ad made, a campaign objective optimising for the wrong action, or an audience that clicks out of curiosity rather than intent.

Is your conversion tracking actually working?

Check tracking before you change anything else. An account with broken conversion tracking cannot be optimised, only guessed at.

This is the single most common cause we find, and it masquerades as every other problem. If conversions are firing inaccurately or not at all, Meta's algorithm is optimising toward a signal that does not reflect reality, and every subsequent change you make is based on noise.

When we rebuilt a sauna retailer's paid account, tracking was fixed before anything was judged. With clean measurement in place the restructure moved ROI up 87%. The tracking work was not the optimisation; it was what made optimisation possible.

Separate the three possible failures before changing anything: nobody clicks, people click and leave, or people convert and the business never contacts them. Each has a different fix and only one of them is the ad.

Click-through rate, landing page behaviour and lead response time answer that question in about ten minutes, and most accounts skip straight past it to new creative.

Does your landing page continue the promise the ad made?

A click is a question. The landing page has to answer the same question the ad asked, in the same words, above the fold.

If the ad offers a free roof inspection and the landing page opens with a general company overview, the visitor has to re-orient before they can act. Most do not bother.

Page quality also affects how often your ads serve at all. In a US health insurance campaign we built the landing page and site first, before any keyword work, specifically because page quality score determines impression share in that category.

Creative fatigue has a signature: frequency climbing while click-through rate falls on the same audience. That is a supply problem, not a messaging problem, and the fix is more creative rather than better creative.

A genuinely weak ad looks different. It underperforms from the start, at low frequency, which means the message never landed in the first place.

Is the campaign objective optimising for the right action?

Meta delivers what you ask for. Ask for clicks and you will get clicks from people who like clicking.

Objective selection is a bigger lever than most advertisers treat it as. We ran four campaigns for one fashion brand against the same audience and market, and the objectives behaved completely differently: 12,813 Messenger conversations at $0.04, 121 website purchases at $0.71, and an organic Reel that reached 333,110 views on no spend at all.

Same audience, three wildly different cost structures. If you are optimising for traffic and expecting leads, the objective is the problem.

The page is the most commonly misdiagnosed cause. A click costs money and a page that loads slowly, asks for too much, or does not repeat the ad's promise discards it after you have paid.

Check the match first. If the ad offered a free roof inspection and the page is a general services homepage, no amount of ad optimisation will recover that gap.

Is your audience interested or just curious?

Cheap clicks from a broad audience often mean you are reaching people who find the ad interesting but have no intention of buying.

A full-funnel structure fixes this by separating the two jobs. Build the warm audience with one campaign, then run lead ads only against people who have already shown interest.

That structure produced 64 B2B leads at $5.08, 63% below Meta's benchmark, from a $100 awareness layer that reached 43,927 people first.

Then check what happens to the leads. On the roofing campaign the cost per lead was only useful because the leads were worked; the same ads with a two-day callback would have produced the same report and no revenue.

A pipeline that shows which leads were never contacted usually finds the problem faster than any creative test.

What should you check first?

Work in this order: tracking, objective, landing page, then audience and creative.

The order matters because each step makes the next one measurable. Changing creative while tracking is broken produces a result you cannot read.

How long should you wait before judging?

Long enough to leave the learning phase, and no longer.

Judging an ad set in its first days measures the exploration period rather than the ad. Judging it after a month of underperformance wastes three weeks of budget.

Decide the review point and the threshold before launching. Doing it afterwards invites the result to set the standard.

What should you change first?

The thing you can measure the effect of.

Changing creative, audience, budget and page together is the default response to disappointment and the reason nothing is ever learned. One variable, one window, one reading.

If the diagnosis points at the page, start there, because it affects every channel pointing at it rather than one ad set.
