Quality Score: What Actually Moves It

By Digital Picto October 19, 2026 9 min read

Quality Score combines three things: expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. Most advertisers rewrite ads and ignore the page, which is why their scores stay flat. In our campaigns we build the landing page first precisely because page quality raises impression share without raising bids.

What are the three components?

Expected click-through rate, ad relevance to the search query, and landing page experience.

Each is scored as below average, average, or above average, and each can be diagnosed separately in the Google Ads interface.

Treating Quality Score as one opaque number is why it feels unfixable. Treating it as three diagnoses makes it actionable.

Each component is visible as a column in the keyword view, and most accounts have never turned those columns on. Doing so converts a vague complaint about cost into a list of specific keywords with a specific diagnosis.

Read them per keyword, not per account. An average score hides the two keywords consuming a third of the budget at below-average relevance.

Why does the landing page matter so much?

Because it is the component most advertisers never touch, which makes it the cheapest one to improve.

On the health insurance campaign we built and optimised the landing page before launch, specifically to raise page quality score and win more impressions in an expensive auction.

Relevance, load speed and a match between the ad's promise and the page's headline all feed this component.

Three things do most of the work: the page headline echoes the query, the page loads fast on a phone, and the action the ad promised is available without scrolling or hunting.

The echo matters more than people expect. A visitor who clicked an ad about emergency repair and lands on a general services page has to re-orient, and a measurable share of them leave instead.

How does ad group structure affect relevance?

Tight ad groups raise relevance because every ad can speak directly to one search intent.

This is the core argument for Single Themed Ad Groups: one theme per group means one message, and one message that matches the query.

A broad ad group forces generic copy, and generic copy earns average relevance at best.

Expected click-through rate responds to the same structural work from the other direction. A specific ad in a specific group earns clicks at a higher rate, which feeds the component that then lowers your cost.

This is why the three components are not independent levers. Structure improves relevance, relevance improves click-through, and the page improves the third, so the ordering of the work is close to fixed.

What does a better score buy you?

More impressions and lower cost for the same bid.

Quality Score is an input to Ad Rank, so improving it is functionally a discount on every click you win.

Our paid advertising work treats it as structural rather than cosmetic: structure, page and copy, in that order.

The discount is real but it is not the main prize. The larger effect is impression share: a better score wins auctions you were previously not competitive in at all, which shows up as volume rather than efficiency.

In an expensive category that is the difference between a campaign that spends its budget and one that cannot find traffic at a price it can justify.

What does not move Quality Score?

Pausing keywords, raising bids, and rewriting ads cosmetically.

Pausing a low-scoring keyword removes the symptom and keeps the cause, and the history follows the keyword if you re-enable it later.

Bids affect position, not score. Advertisers who respond to a low score by bidding up are paying a premium to keep the problem.

Is it worth tracking over time?

Track the components, not the number.

The composite score moves for reasons outside your control, including competitor behaviour in the same auction. The component ratings tell you whether your own work landed.

Record them before and after a structural change. That is the only way to know whether the restructure or the page rebuild did the work.
