Does B2B Work on Facebook Ads? What We Found

By Digital Picto October 17, 2026 9 min read

B2B does work on Facebook Ads, but not with the structure most advertisers use. Running lead ads cold to a business audience is expensive because you are asking a stranger for contact details before they know who you are. In two published B2B campaigns, building the audience first produced leads at $5.08 and $12.23, both well inside what the contract values could absorb.

Why do B2B Facebook campaigns usually cost more?

Because most B2B advertisers skip the step that makes the lead cheap: they run lead ads to people who have never encountered the brand.

A B2B purchase usually involves a larger commitment, a longer consideration period and often more than one decision-maker. Asking for a form fill on first contact fights all three.

The fix is structural rather than creative. Separate the job of introducing the business from the job of collecting the lead, and let the first one make the second one cheaper.

The mismatch is intent. Nobody opens a social feed to procure software, so a campaign that asks for a demo booking is asking for a decision the moment cannot support.

What the channel can do is create awareness among people who will search later, and capture the ones already considering. Both require an offer sized to a casual moment.

What does a full-funnel B2B structure look like?

An awareness campaign builds a warm pool, then a lead campaign retargets only that pool.

For Nevada Access Solutions, a company selling packages above $1,000, the awareness layer ran first on $100 and reached 43,927 people. The lead campaign then ran against that warm audience and returned 64 website leads at $5.08.

That figure came in 63% below what Meta itself benchmarks for similar ad sets. The structure was doing the work: three ad sets and six creatives for reach, then six lead creatives tested against the audience that reach created.

Offer something with standalone value that does not require a meeting: a teardown, a benchmark, a template, a short diagnostic. The exchange has to feel fair for a thirty-second decision.

Then qualify afterwards rather than at the form. A longer form filters out the curious and also the busy, and the busy are the buyers.

Can B2B campaigns work on a deadline?

Yes, if you stop trying to find the single winning audience and instead run several clean campaigns at once against different segments.

Tombizmerch sells World Cup merchandise to embroidery shops, clothing brands and retail owners. A World Cup is a fixed window, so there was no version where we spent three weeks testing and scaled afterwards.

Three separate campaigns launched simultaneously, one per segment, and the market decided which answered. Seven days produced 64 qualified B2B leads at $12.23 average on $782.84, with three moving into active sales conversations.

Targeting by job title alone is usually weaker than targeting by the problem. Titles are self-reported, inconsistent across companies, and frequently stale; behaviour and interest signals age better.

Retargeting is where B2B on Meta is most reliably efficient, because it reaches people who already demonstrated intent somewhere you could measure.

How do you judge B2B lead quality?

Count conversations, not form fills. A B2B lead that never reaches a call was not a lead.

In the Tombizmerch campaign we tracked how many of the 64 leads moved into live sales conversations, because that is the number that determines whether the campaign paid.

This is also why cost per lead alone is a weak measure in B2B. A $12 lead that books a call beats a $4 lead that does not.

Measure to the pipeline, not to the platform. A cost per lead that looks high against consumer benchmarks can be excellent against a deal size that justifies it, and the reverse is also true.

That requires the Conversions API and a CRM that reports back which leads became opportunities. Without it you are optimising toward form fills.

Is Facebook better than LinkedIn for B2B?

They solve different problems. Facebook reaches business owners in their personal feed at a lower cost; LinkedIn reaches them by job title at a higher one.

For owner-operated businesses, trades and small firms, the decision-maker is often reachable far more cheaply on Meta than on LinkedIn, because you are targeting interests and behaviours rather than paying the premium for firmographic data.

Our paid advertising service picks the platform from who the buyer is and how they can be reached, not from a general rule about B2B.

How long is the feedback loop?

Longer than the optimisation window, which is the central practical problem.

If deals close in ninety days, campaign decisions made on thirty days of data are made on leading indicators. Choose the indicator deliberately, usually a qualified conversation rather than a form fill.

Send that qualification event back to the platform as the conversion. Optimising toward qualified conversations rather than raw leads is the single highest-value change available to most B2B accounts.

Where does this sit against search?

Search captures intent that already exists; Meta creates the awareness that produces it.

Running Meta alone in B2B usually disappoints, because it fills the top of a funnel with no capture layer underneath. Running search alone caps out at existing demand.

The combination is what we build in paid advertising engagements, with attribution that can see both rather than crediting whichever touched last.
