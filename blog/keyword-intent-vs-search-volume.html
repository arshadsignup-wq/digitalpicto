Why Ranking for 669 Keywords Earned Almost Nothing

By Digital Picto October 23, 2026 8 min read

Ranking for more keywords is not the goal, and a site can prove it by ranking widely and earning nothing. One account we audited had 669 keywords ranking and roughly 670 visits a month, almost none of which attached to anything a customer would actually buy. Intent is the variable that decides whether traffic converts, and volume tells you nothing about it.

What does buying intent actually look like?

A query where the searcher has already decided to buy and is choosing who from.

Compare 'what is a grazing platter' with 'party platter delivery near me'. The first is research; the second is a purchase in progress. Both may have volume; only one has a customer attached.

Informational queries still have a role for authority and AI citation, but they should not be the whole strategy if the site needs revenue.

High-volume head terms are usually a mix of intents, which is why they convert poorly even when you rank. 'Email marketing' contains students, job seekers, competitors and a few buyers, and you pay for all of them.

Intent-specific queries have smaller numbers and a single population. A hundred searches where everyone wants to hire someone is worth more than ten thousand where one in two hundred does.

Why is a well-ranking site sometimes the harder starting point?

Because the obvious diagnosis, 'we need more visibility', is wrong, and acting on it makes things worse.

Pretty Party Platters was already ranking when we audited it in October 2025. The audit found the problem was quality, not visibility: of 45 pages crawled, 2 were healthy and 41 had issues.

Adding more content to that site would have added more low-intent rankings. The work that moved traffic from 670 to 2,800 a month was re-aiming at intent, not publishing more.

Read the results page before deciding. If a query returns guides and definitions, it is informational however commercial it sounds, and a service page will not rank for it no matter how well optimised.

That check also tells you what the page needs to be. The format of what already ranks is the clearest statement available about what the searcher wanted.

How do you find high-intent keywords?

Start from what you sell and how buyers describe it, then validate against the search results rather than a volume column.

Look at who currently ranks. If the first page is product and service pages, the query has commercial intent. If it is definitions and listicles, it is informational.

A low-volume query that returns service pages is usually worth more than a high-volume query that returns encyclopaedia entries.

Modifiers are the fastest signal: 'how to' and 'what is' are informational, 'best' and 'vs' are comparative, 'near me' and 'pricing' are transactional, and 'free' and 'template' usually indicate someone who will not buy.

Build the page for the intent, then measure against the intent. An informational page judged on direct conversions will look like a failure while it feeds every other page on the site.

Does low volume mean not worth targeting?

No. Narrow, specific queries convert at far higher rates and are usually far easier to win.

A new site with no authority should bias deliberately toward long-tail and local queries, because those are winnable now rather than theoretically.

Specific queries also map cleanly onto individual pages, which is what makes them rankable in the first place.

Volume still matters as a tiebreaker between queries of equal intent, and as a sanity check that a topic has demand at all. The error is using it as the primary sort.

Our SEO work sorts by intent and competitive realism first, which is also why the keyword list is usually shorter and the reporting usually better.

How should intent change your page structure?

One intent per page. A page trying to serve research and purchase at once serves neither.

If a term needs explaining before it can be sold, that is two pages: an explainer that earns the ranking and a service page that takes the order, linked.

Our SEO programmes map every target keyword to exactly one page for this reason, which also prevents pages competing with each other.

How do you cover informational intent profitably?

Answer it fully, then link down to the page that converts.

Informational content earns authority and citations, and it is increasingly how AI assistants decide whom to name. Treating it as unmonetisable misreads what it does.

The link matters. An informational page with no obvious next step has done the work and collected none of the value.

What about zero-volume keywords?

Some of the best queries report no volume at all.

Keyword tools round small numbers to zero, and highly specific commercial queries, a particular service in a particular city, often sit below the reporting threshold while converting at a far higher rate than anything above it.

Mine the search terms report from paid campaigns and your own site search for these. They are invisible to competitors using the same tools you are.
