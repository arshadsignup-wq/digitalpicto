How to Get Your Site Cited by AI Assistants

By Digital Picto October 22, 2026 10 min read

AI assistants cite pages that answer a specific question cleanly, in one self-contained passage, on a page they can crawl. On a US catering site we took through ten months of SEO work, 262 pages ended up cited across ChatGPT, AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini. The work that produced those citations was the same work that produced the rankings: intent-matched content, structured clearly.

What actually makes a page quotable?

A passage that answers one question completely without needing the paragraphs around it for context.

An assistant lifting an answer cannot include your whole page. It needs a block of text that stands alone and is unambiguously about the question asked.

In practice that means opening each section with a direct answer of one to three sentences, then elaborating underneath. The elaboration is for the reader; the opening is what gets quoted.

Citation is an extraction problem before it is an authority problem. A page that is trusted but structured as a continuous argument gives an assistant nothing to lift, and a clearer page from a weaker domain gets named instead.

This is unusually good news for smaller sites. Structure is a discipline anyone can apply, unlike domain authority.

Does AI visibility require different work from SEO?

Mostly no. The same intent-matched, well-structured content earns both, which is why they tend to move together.

For Pretty Party Platters, organic traffic went from 670 to 2,800 monthly visits over ten months while AI citations reached 262 pages. Those were not separate projects.

The starting problem was instructive: the site already ranked for 669 keywords and still went nowhere, because almost none of those keywords attached to anything a customer would buy. Fixing intent fixed both channels at once.

Specificity is what makes a passage preferable to the four others saying the same thing. A named figure, a named mechanism, or a concrete order of operations survives summarisation; a hedged generality does not.

That is also why first-hand material wins. A result you measured, with the conditions attached, cannot be sourced from anywhere else.

Do you need to let AI crawlers in?

Yes. If your robots.txt blocks them, no amount of good content will be cited.

Check explicitly for GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended. Many sites block these without realising, often through a default rule.

Allowing them is a deliberate decision about whether you want to be quoted. For most service businesses, being the source an assistant cites is worth more than the click it occasionally replaces.

Consistency across the web matters more than it does for classic SEO. Assistants aggregate, so a business whose service description, location and specialism differ between its own site, its directory listings and its profiles produces a blurred answer.

Fix the simple version first: the same name, the same description, the same claims, everywhere you appear.

How does page structure help?

Clear headings phrased as real questions let an assistant find the passage that matches the query.

Write H2s the way people actually ask, then answer immediately underneath. Name the entities explicitly: the business, the service, the location. Assistants resolve pronouns poorly.

FAQ sections with proper FAQPage schema give a second, highly structured route to the same answers, which is why we add them to every page we publish.

Check your own standing by asking the questions a buyer would ask, in several assistants, and recording who gets named. That list is your competitive set in this channel, and it is often not the one you assumed.

Repeat it monthly. It moves, and it moves for reasons you can sometimes act on.

How do you measure AI visibility?

Track cited pages and brand mentions rather than positions, because there is no ranking to hold.

Tools reporting AI citation counts give a directional read. The more reliable signal is whether assistants return your business when asked the questions your buyers ask.

Our SEO service treats citations as a reporting line alongside rankings and traffic, because increasingly the answer appears before the result.

Does llms.txt do anything?

For some systems, possibly. For Google Search, no.

It costs almost nothing to publish and it is not a ranking mechanism. Treating it as the strategy is the mistake; treating it as one small piece of discoverability hygiene is reasonable.

The crawler allowlist in robots.txt is the part that genuinely matters. A site blocking AI crawlers cannot be cited by them, and some sites do this unintentionally through a broad disallow rule.

How does this differ from traditional SEO?

The work overlaps heavily; the measurement does not.

Clean structure, real expertise and clear answers serve both. What changes is that success may be a mention with no click, which existing analytics will not show you.

So the metric has to be built deliberately: citation checks, branded search volume, and direct traffic that arrives already informed. Our SEO work tracks the first of those alongside rankings.
