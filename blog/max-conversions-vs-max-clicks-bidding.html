Max Conversions or Max Clicks: Which Bidding?

Digital Picto October 26, 2026 8 min read

Maximum Clicks buys the most traffic for your budget. Maximum Conversions buys the most conversions, but it can only do that if conversion tracking is accurate and the account has enough conversion history to learn from. Start on clicks only while you are building that history, then switch.

When is Maximum Clicks the right choice?

Only in a brand new account with no conversion data, and only temporarily.

Smart bidding needs signal. With zero conversions recorded, there is nothing for the algorithm to optimise toward.

The risk is staying there. Maximum Clicks will happily spend a budget on traffic that never converts, because traffic is exactly what you asked for.

There is one other legitimate use: diagnosing whether a problem is demand or conversion. If Maximum Clicks cannot spend the budget, the issue is reach, bids or targeting. If it spends easily but nothing converts, the issue is the page or the offer.

Used that way it is an instrument, not a strategy. The cost of running it for a week to answer that question is almost always lower than the cost of guessing.

What has to be true before switching to conversions?

Conversion tracking has to be correct, and it has to be measuring the right event.

A campaign optimising toward a mis-fired conversion tag will get very good at producing the wrong outcome.

Our guide to conversion tracking setup covers the verification steps before any bid strategy change.

Verify three things before switching: the tag fires once and only once on the real success state, the conversion counts in the interface match what the CRM or inbox actually received, and the conversion action is marked as primary.

That last one catches a surprising number of accounts. A campaign optimising toward a secondary action will get very efficient at generating page views of the thank-you page while the phone stays quiet.

What results does conversion bidding produce?

On correctly tracked accounts, substantially better conversion rates than click-maximising equivalents.

We ran Maximum Conversions bidding on the health insurance campaign and reached a 9.90% conversion rate at $2.33 average cost per click.

On the process server campaign the same approach rebuilt a stalled account into 142 leads, with ROI up 31% and cost per click down 17%.

Both of those campaigns shared a trait beyond the bid strategy: a landing page built before launch, and tight thematic ad groups feeding it. Smart bidding amplifies whatever conversion rate the page already has; it does not create one.

This is the honest limit of the comparison. The bid strategy is worth a meaningful improvement on a well-built funnel and almost nothing on a broken one.

What about target CPA?

Add a target once you know what a conversion is actually worth, not before.

Setting an aggressive target CPA too early starves the campaign of volume before it has learned anything.

Let Maximum Conversions find the natural cost first, then constrain it if the economics require.

Set the target from the economics, not from the current cost per acquisition. If a customer is worth $400 and closes one time in four, the ceiling on a lead is $100, and a target set at $40 because that is what last month happened to produce will suppress volume you could profitably buy.

What happens during the learning period?

Performance becomes unreliable for a while, and the usual mistake is to react to it.

Every bid strategy change restarts learning. Daily cost per conversion will swing in both directions while the system re-explores the auction, and judging the strategy inside that window is how good changes get reverted.

Decide the review date before making the change, and hold to it unless spend runs away entirely. Changing strategy again mid-learning simply restarts the clock.

Should budget and bidding change together?

No. Change one variable at a time or you will not know which one did anything.

A simultaneous budget increase and strategy switch produces a result you cannot attribute, which means you cannot repeat it. Sequence them, with a learning period between.

The same applies to creative and page changes. Our paid advertising work keeps structural changes separated in time so each one can be read.
