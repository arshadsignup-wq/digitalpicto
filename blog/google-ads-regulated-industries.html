Running Google Ads in a Regulated Industry

Digital Picto November 8, 2026 9 min read

In regulated categories such as health insurance, finance and legal services, the constraint is not creativity but permission. Knowing precisely what may and may not be claimed lets you write copy that resonates without triggering disapprovals that stall a campaign. In US health insurance we reached a 9.90% conversion rate and 8.72% click-through rate working inside those rules.

Why do regulated campaigns stall?

Disapprovals, which stop delivery entirely rather than merely reducing it.

An ad group where every ad is disapproved cannot serve. The account looks enabled and spends nothing.

Repeated policy violations can also affect the account beyond the individual ad, which makes guessing an expensive way to learn the rules.

There is a second, slower failure: an ad that clears review but makes a claim the landing page cannot support. Those get caught later, often after spend, and the correction is more expensive than a disapproval would have been.

Certification requirements add a third. Some categories cannot serve at all until the advertiser is verified, and discovering that after building the campaign wastes the build rather than the budget.

How does compliance become an advantage?

Because most competitors either overclaim and get blocked, or underclaim and say nothing persuasive.

Understanding the boundary lets you write the strongest permitted claim rather than the safest possible one.

In the health insurance campaign, that understanding is what produced copy that resonated with policyholders while clearing review.

The boundary is usually about specificity and guarantees rather than subject matter. You frequently cannot promise an outcome, name a price as universal, or imply endorsement, and you frequently can describe the process, the coverage and who it suits.

That is a writable brief. The strongest permitted claim is almost always concrete and procedural, which happens to be more persuasive than the vague superlative the policy blocked.

What structure works in competitive regulated auctions?

Tight thematic ad groups, a heavily optimised landing page, and bidding aimed at conversions rather than clicks.

We used Maximum Conversions bidding with Single Themed Ad Groups, and built the landing page first to raise page quality score and win more impressions.

In an expensive auction, impression share won through quality is cheaper than impression share bought through bids.

The page carries the compliance weight as well as the conversion weight. Disclosures, licensing information and the absence of claims the ad did not make all live there, and a page that contradicts the ad is a review risk in both directions.

That is part of why we build the page first. It sets what the ads are allowed to say, rather than the ads setting what the page has to defend.

What should you do before launching?

Read the category policy, build the page, then write the ads against both.

Writing ads first and discovering the policy afterwards is the expensive order.

Our paid advertising service treats policy review as part of setup in these categories rather than a reaction to a disapproval.

Keep a written record of why each ad is worded the way it is. In a regulated account, the person who inherits it will otherwise 'improve' a carefully worded line and trigger a disapproval nobody can explain.

Budget for the review cycle in the launch timeline. Policy review is not instant, and a launch date that assumes same-day approval will slip.

Does this change the targeting rules?

Yes, and it is the part advertisers most often miss.

Several regulated categories restrict personalised targeting, which means audience strategies that are routine elsewhere are unavailable. Health in particular limits what you may target on.

The practical consequence is that keyword and query intent carry more of the load, which raises the value of tight ad group structure and a disciplined negative keyword list.

What should the first week look like?

Small budget, close monitoring, and a readiness to rewrite rather than appeal.

Launching at full budget into an unverified policy position risks spending while a disapproval propagates. A controlled start surfaces the problems cheaply.

Appeal only when the ad genuinely complies. Appealing an ad that does not is a week spent waiting for an answer you already know, and the rewrite would have taken an afternoon.
